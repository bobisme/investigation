# Dogfood: why repair index is slow, and which fix to adopt (W14.10)

This note records one real investigation run with the shipped binary on 2026-11-24 (bone bn-6n1p, gap-audit row G10). The question came from the project itself: PERF.08 (bn-171a) reported that `investigation repair index` took 38 s at 3.8k objects and "over 10 min" at 41.6k. Two cases were run. An explanatory case asked why. An evaluative case asked which fix to adopt. Both cases were closed with a Retrospective and exported. Midway through the explanatory case, a fresh hosted agent continued the case from the handoff alone. This is a development check by the builder's own agent. It does not prove the tool works for other teams.

The case workspace is kept outside the repository, at `~/.local/share/investigation-dogfood/perf08` (mode 0700), on the owner's machine. Evidence was imported from a sibling directory, also mode 0700. Its files are measurements, profiles, code excerpts, a prototype diff and test logs from this repository. None of the case data, exported files or evidence bytes are in this tree. This note cites only case IDs, export IDs and the results.

## The answers

**Explanatory.** The 38 s that PERF.08 reports was caused by index syncs on every event. PERF.09 (101917a9, ADR 0015) already fixed it.
- On 439ad79, a rebuild did about 4 syncs per event: 4,376 syncs at 60 cases and 46,856 at 650. CPU was about 2.5% of wall time.
- The same rebuild took 38.6–39.1 s on ext4 and 0.27 s on tmpfs.
- On main (988ae6b3), a rebuild does 3 syncs. It takes 0.26 s at 60 cases, 0.56–0.60 s at 130 and 2.52–2.63 s at 650. That is 61–72 µs per object, and it grows linearly.
- The superlinear growth was not reproduced. Per-object time followed sync latency, and the 41.6k figure came from a run that was stopped.
- The time left on main is CPU, from a frame-pointer perf profile:
  - applying records: 65–73%;
  - verification: 13–18%;
  - dropping the verified replay: about 12%.
- Caveats:
  - The conclusion reads as `needs_review` (see F11).
  - All runs used one machine and generated workspaces.
  - The old build was measured only at 60, 130 and 650 cases.

**Evaluative.** Adopt the bundle as a follow-up to PERF.08, not in this bone. The bundle has four parts:
1. a statement cache;
2. create each record table once per rebuild connection;
3. one outer transaction for the rebuild's unpublished file;
4. free the replay with FreeLater.

A prototype was measured and not landed. On the 650-case workspace (n=3, interleaved), the bundle took 1.34–1.35 s against 2.25–2.39 s on main, so it is 1.7x faster. Each part alone gave less:
- cache: about −20%;
- one transaction: −17%;
- FreeLater: −10%;
- cache plus one transaction: −33%.

A reviewer then found that these timings came from a release binary built before the prototype's last edit. A release build of the final source was timed again, at load average 50–70. The bundle took 2.16–2.42 s against 3.72–3.74 s for main, about 38% less, and the index was again identical. Absolute times are not comparable between the two runs; the ordering is.

Checks on the prototype:
- The index rows were identical to main's at 130 and 650 cases.
- The store suite passed.
- The first prototype failed one existing test, because the table memo also applied to the live index and a rolled-back CREATE TABLE left it wrong. Scoping the memo to the rebuild connection fixed that.

Two options were rejected:
- Skip or reuse verification. It fails ADR 0015's "repair index verifies every case".
- An incremental rebuild. It would let a torn row survive the repair.

Hosted reviewers raised two objections. After them, the Decision makes a test of the bulk path a landing precondition, and it no longer closes PERF.08. The explanatory conclusion is not independently reviewed, so closing the bone is left to bn-171a's owner. The numbers and the recommendation are posted on bn-171a.

## What the tool did well

- **Ingest and quoting.** `start --attach` and `source add` kept every artifact with a digest. Selector quotes forced each observation to cite an exact line of the evidence. When I misquoted, the tool refused the quote and did not record a paraphrase.
- **Rival hypotheses.**
  - Four rival hypotheses were recorded: syncs, verification, SQLite statement work and data growth.
  - `assessment.add` needs an expectation for every live rival, so each observation had to say what each rival predicted.
  - This rejected "verification is the cost" and "data growth" on evidence, not by omission.
- **`next` and `blindspots`.** Both pointed at real gaps:
  - an open hard criterion cell;
  - observations without a validity judgment;
  - a missing Retrospective.
- **The handoff.** It was enough for a successor with no other context to continue the work.
- **Review gates.** Accepting a Decision needed a supporting review from a current validation. Two independent reviews found real defects: a hard condition stated as met while its test did not exist, and a closure claim resting on an unreviewed conclusion. The gate kept both errors out of the accepted record.
- **Exports.** The report, handoff and decision record are exported with a head digest, so this note can cite them without copying them.

## The handoff

At snapshot 12 of the explanatory case, I exported a handoff. Its stop condition asked for the scaling and profile observations, and assessments against all four hypotheses. No conclusion was asked for. The successor was a fresh hosted Claude Code session on Haiku, run as `agent:successor-haiku`:
- It ran in a bwrap sandbox with a fresh home and Bash only.
- It had an empty strict MCP configuration and no skills, CLAUDE.md or AGENTS.md.
- It reused the token-copy and cleanup functions of `scripts/run-first-use-hosted.py`.

The successor finished in 409.5 s: 49 turns, 48 Bash calls (3 of them errors), USD 0.42.
- It recorded 9 observations, 2 `measurement.assess` batches and 4 assessments, one per hypothesis.
- It found the two sources without observations by itself, and `check` passed.
- It stopped as told, without concluding.
- The main session then read its records and concluded.

Where it stumbled:
- The handoff cites records without their current revision. Its first assessments failed with a revision conflict, because it had guessed revision 1.
- It read "over 10 min" in the objective as a fact.
- It cited a measurement judged `partial` as support and was refused.
- Its `search` for observations found nothing.

These findings are in DF.04, DF.05 and DF.08.

The evaluative Decision needed a supporting review, so six more hosted Haiku sessions ran in the same sandbox as reviewers. Each ran once, for 160–240 s:
1. Review 1 objected, with a correct material objection (`obj_01a2107f-0264-7522-ab9b-48df4374a547`). A hard condition was stated as met, but its test did not exist. I resolved it by revising a comparison and proposing a corrected Decision.
2. Review 2 was blinded, and it was inconclusive, because the packet hides the choice.
3. Review 3 read the Decision and supported it. It used review 2's actor, so the two reviews fall in one dependence group. Marking the option eligible then staled the Decision (F16).
4. Review 4, with its own actor, supported it. Accept then refused, because the chosen option's comparisons pinned its old revision (F16).
5. Review 5 objected, with a correct material objection (`obj_01a2108f-27d2-7700-a3b4-fe84d9e81a90`): the Decision closed PERF.08 on an unreviewed conclusion. It also found that the timed binary predated the final source. I rebuilt and re-timed the prototype and revised the Decision.
6. Review 6 supported the Decision, with caveats. The Decision was accepted on this review (`rev_01a21097-fbf3-7953-abe7-6d708bf25c35`).

The two objections were the most useful output of the whole exercise. Both were real defects in my record, and neither was caught by `blindspots`. Reviews 1, 2, 4 and 6 each reported CLI friction of their own:
- the record envelope fields that `schema review.submit` does not show;
- the kinds that `materials_examined` accepts;
- the size of the packet.

These are filed under DF.02 and DF.07.

`verify_clean` passed after every hosted run: the probe, the successor and the six reviewers. No token copy is left under the runner's home directory, and no credential is left in any output directory.

## Limitations

- **One team, chosen cases.** The builder's own agent ran both cases on a question from the project. The question suits the tool well: local files, measurements and code.
- **Familiarity.** I know the schema well enough to read `$defs`. A new user would hit the F-findings harder, and the hosted agents did.
- **Models.** The successor and the six reviewers were one model (Haiku), with one run each. Their output is evidence of what can be done from the handoff, not a rate.
- **Measurements.** All numbers come from one shared machine: load average 12–37 for the explanatory runs and the first prototype timings, and 50–70 for the re-run. The orderings held in every interleaved round; absolute times did not.
- **The prototype.** It was not reviewed as code. Its durability argument is by inspection of ADR 0015, not by fault injection.

## Deliberately not changed

- **No code.** Neither PERF.08's fix nor any friction finding was changed in this bone. The prototype stays outside the tree, and the fix is a comment on bn-171a.
- **The gap audit.** `notes/release/2026-11-24-gap-audit.md` is a dated snapshot. Row G10 is left for the next audit, which can cite this note.
- **The PERF.08 bone.** bn-171a was not closed or resized. That choice belongs to whoever takes the fix.
